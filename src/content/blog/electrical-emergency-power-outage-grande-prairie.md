If you notice a burning smell, sparking outlet, half your house dark, or water near a panel, shut off the main breaker and call a licensed electrician right away.

## What should I do if I smell burning near an outlet?
A burning odor from an outlet or switch is a clear sign that something is wrong inside the wiring. **Never ignore it** even if the outlet looks fine. Turn off the circuit at the breaker before touching anything. Unplug any devices that might be plugged into the affected outlet. If the smell persists after the breaker is off, leave the area and call an emergency electrician. 

- Cut power at the breaker for that circuit.  
- Do not reset the breaker until a professional inspects it.  
- Keep children and pets away from the outlet.  

You can find fast help from our team by visiting [emergency](/services/emergency).

## Why is half my house without power and how can I tell if it’s a tripped breaker?
When a portion of your home loses electricity while the rest stays on, the issue is usually a tripped breaker or a faulty circuit. First, check the main breaker in your panel; it will be in the middle or off position if tripped. Then look at the individual breakers for the affected rooms. A breaker that is halfway between on and off is often the culprit. If resetting it restores power and the problem returns, there may be a deeper fault that needs a panel upgrade.

- Inspect the main breaker for a tripped position.  
- Reset the breaker only if it stays on.  
- If it trips again, call a professional for a detailed inspection.  

For more details on panel work, see our [panel upgrades](/services/panel-upgrades) page.

## When is a sparking panel an emergency that requires immediate power cutoff?
Sparking, arcing, or a buzzing sound from the electrical panel indicates a serious fault that can cause a fire. If you see bright arcs, hear crackling, or notice a metallic smell, treat it as an emergency. Shut off the main breaker immediately to stop electricity from flowing through the panel. Do not attempt to open the panel yourself unless you are a licensed electrician. 

- See bright arcs or hear crackling inside the panel.  
- Smell of ozone or burning plastic near the panel.  
- Breaker won’t stay on after you try to reset it.  

If any of these signs appear, call our emergency team right away. You can reach us through the contact form on our site.

## How do I protect my home from electrical fires during a cold Grande Prairie winter?
Winter in the Peace Country brings low temperatures, block heaters, and high demand on well pumps and pressure systems. These loads can stress older wiring, especially on acreages where circuits may be older. Use outdoor‑rated extension cords for holiday lights and keep heat sources away from curtains or stored paper. Have a qualified electrician inspect well pumps and pressure tanks before the freeze sets in to ensure connections are secure and corrosion‑free.

- Use cords rated for outdoor and sub‑zero use.  
- Check that all outdoor outlets have GFCI protection.  
- Schedule a pre‑winter inspection of well and pressure system wiring.  

## What should I do if water gets near my electrical panel or outlets?
Water and electricity do not mix. If you see moisture near a panel, outlets, or switch plates, turn off the main breaker before doing anything else. Do not touch wet switches or attempt to dry them yourself. Even a small amount of water can create a short circuit that damages equipment or starts a fire. After the power is off, call a professional to assess the damage and perform any necessary repairs.

- Cut power at the main breaker immediately.  
- Keep the area dry and avoid using the affected circuits.  
- Arrange for a licensed inspection before restoring power.  

For any urgent concerns, you can get in touch with us via the [contact](/contact) page.

When planning larger projects like panel upgrades or new installations, remember that Alberta requires a permit from the local Safety Codes authority. More information on the permitting process is available on the [Alberta Safety Codes](https://www.alberta.ca/safety-codes.aspx) website.

**Bottom line:** Treat any burning smell, sparking panel, partial power loss, or water intrusion as an electrical emergency. Shut off the main breaker, stay safe, and call a licensed professional. Our team is ready to help you keep your home powered safely through the long Alberta winters and bright summer days.
